\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Academic researchers lose 40--60\% of their time to non-research overhead: searching scattered files, manually summarizing papers, formatting citations, and collaborating over email attachments, routinely juggling four or more disconnected tools while legacy reference managers (Zotero, Mendeley) offer essentially no artificial intelligence and only weak collaboration. A survey of 32 students and faculty across universities in Bangladesh, conducted for the ScholarFlow Software Requirements Specification, confirmed strong demand for a dedicated solution: 75\% of respondents express at least moderate need, current tool satisfaction averages only 3.28 out of 5, and 100\% are interested in AI features --- including 65.6\% selecting AI-generated summaries, 62.5\% selecting AI-powered Q\&A, and 68.8\% selecting AI-generated key points --- with note-taking pain (46.9\%), finding saved papers (40.6\%), and organization (34.4\%) as the dominant problems. This report presents \textbf{ScholarFlow}, a full-stack AI-powered research paper collaboration hub consolidating the full lifecycle (\emph{upload $\rightarrow$ organize $\rightarrow$ read $\rightarrow$ annotate $\rightarrow$ write $\rightarrow$ cite $\rightarrow$ collaborate $\rightarrow$ publish}) into one secure, student-friendly platform: S3-backed paper storage with automatic AI metadata extraction, full-text plus pgvector semantic search, inline annotations and notebooks, a TipTap academic editor with LaTeX math, templates, version history and server-rendered export, nine citation formats, role-based workspaces with invitations, granular sharing, live discussions and real-time co-editing, a multi-provider AI layer (Gemini/OpenAI with automatic fallback) powering summaries, Q\&A threads, comparison, translation and literature-review drafts under per-paper access checks, freemium monetization through Stripe with webhook-driven entitlements and a grace-period sweeper, and a complete admin console over users, billing, content, and system health. Built as a fully typed TypeScript monorepo (Next.js frontend, Express REST API, dedicated WebSocket server, PostgreSQL with pgvector, AWS S3) with green quality gates (21 test suites / 59 tests, clean lint and type-check), ScholarFlow demonstrates the end-to-end application of software engineering principles --- requirements analysis, modular architecture, migration discipline, idempotent billing, and measurable verification --- to a scalable, secure, production-grade research platform.
